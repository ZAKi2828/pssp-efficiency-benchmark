Cover Letter for Submission to Computational Biology and Chemistry (Elsevier)

To: The Editor-in-Chief
Computational Biology and Chemistry

Subject: Submission of the research article "Sequence-Only Protein Secondary Structure Prediction under a CPU Budget: Accuracy–Latency Trade-offs of Frozen ESM-2 Probes and Lightweight Non-Pretrained Networks"

Dear Editor,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript by Roaissat Mohamed Zakaria for publication as a Research Article in Computational Biology and Chemistry.

Accurate secondary structure predictors rely on multiple sequence alignments, whose database searches dominate run time. The manuscript presents a reproducible benchmark of five alignment-free predictors whose costs differ by more than two orders of magnitude: a windowed MLP, a hybrid CNN–BiLSTM, and frozen ESM-2 language models (8M and 35M parameters) with linear or MLP heads. The work does not propose a new architecture; its aim is to quantify accuracy–cost trade-offs for users without GPUs.

All models were trained on the NetSurfP-2.0 training set after removing every chain with at least 25% identity to any test chain (MMseqs2), and evaluated with three seeds on CB513, TS115, CASP12, CASP13 and CASP14. The main findings are:

1. Frozen ESM-2 with a small MLP head reached 75.92% (8M) and 80.12% (35M) Q3 on CB513, compared with 67.95% for the windowed MLP and 71.45% for the hybrid CNN–BiLSTM.
2. An MLP head improved on a linear probe by 1.61 points on CB513.
3. On a two-thread CPU, the hybrid network was about 9x faster than the ESM-2 8M pipeline (median of within-round ratios over ten interleaved timing rounds) but recovered only 43.9% of its accuracy gain over the windowed MLP; on GPU the ordering reversed. Every model ran in under 1 GB of RAM.
4. On the small CASP12, CASP13 and CASP14 sets, chain-level confidence intervals were wide (up to 11 points) and the change from CB513 differed between sets (pretrained models dropped more on CASP12 and CASP14, but not on CASP13), suggesting caution when CB513 alone is used to judge language-model-based predictors.

All ten pairwise model differences on CB513 remained significant after Holm correction in residue-level McNemar, protein-level Wilcoxon and protein-level bootstrap tests. The notebook, hyper-parameters, logs and per-residue predictions are publicly available at https://github.com/ZAKI2828/pssp-efficiency-benchmark.

This manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration elsewhere. The author declares no competing interests.
